Learning & Explaining

Explain any concept simply
You are an expert in [SUBJECT]. Explain [CONCEPT] to a [AUDIENCE: e.g. 12-year-old / complete beginner / university student]. Use: - Plain language appropriate for the audience - One concrete real-world analogy - One short example Length: [short (2–3 paragraphs) OR medium (5–6 paragraphs)]
Use for: lessons, study guides, explaining things to family
Step-by-step how-to guide
Write a step-by-step guide for: [TASK] Audience: [WHO WILL READ THIS] Assume they know: [WHAT THEY ALREADY KNOW] Format: Numbered steps. Each step = one action + a brief explanation of why. End with: a one-sentence summary of what they accomplished.
Use for: tutorials, homework help, teaching others

Research & Analysis

Analyse a piece of text
Analyse the following [TYPE: article / essay / report / argument]: <document> [PASTE YOUR TEXT HERE] </document> Provide: 1. Main argument or point (1–2 sentences) 2. Key supporting points (bullet list, max 5) 3. Any assumptions or weaknesses in the argument 4. Your overall assessment: is this convincing? Why? Be concise and honest.
Use for: homework, research, reading comprehension
Compare two things
Compare [OPTION A] vs [OPTION B] for the purpose of: [USE CASE] Structure: - 2-sentence summary of each option - Comparison table with these dimensions: [e.g. cost, ease of use, speed, accuracy] - When to choose A - When to choose B - Your recommendation for my specific situation
Use for: decisions, product comparisons, pros/cons
