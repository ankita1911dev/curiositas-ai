Prompt Engineering — Cheat Sheet

The 4 Core Techniques

Zero-Shot

Ask without examples. Works for clear, simple tasks. Just describe what you want.

Example: "Summarise this in one sentence."
Few-Shot

Give 2–3 examples before your actual question. Locks in the format and tone you want.

Example 1: Input → Output
Example 2: Input → Output
Now do: [your input]
Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

Ask the AI to reason step by step. Dramatically improves accuracy on complex or maths problems.

"Think step by step before answering."
Role Prompting

Give the AI a persona. This shapes vocabulary, expertise level, and tone.

"You are a friendly science teacher
explaining to a 12-year-old.
Explain what gravity is."

Anatomy of a Strong Prompt

PartWhat it doesExample
RoleSets expertise and tone"You are a data scientist…"
ContextGives background info"The reader is a 12-year-old…"
TaskThe clear instruction"Explain gradient descent…"
ConstraintsLimits scope or format"In 3 bullet points, no jargon."
Output formatExact shape of the answer"Return as a JSON object."

Controlling the Output Format

"Respond ONLY in valid JSON. No preamble, no markdown.
Schema: { "answer": string, "confidence": "high"|"medium"|"low" }"
Tip: Use XML-style delimiters to separate your content from your instructions — e.g. wrap pasted text in <document>...</document>. This stops the AI from confusing your content with its instructions.

Common Mistakes (Anti-Patterns)

❌ Too vague

"Write something about AI" — no scope, no format, no audience. The AI will guess, usually wrongly.

❌ Only negatives

"Don't use jargon" → instead say: "Use plain English, maximum 8th-grade reading level."

❌ No output format

Without specifying format, you get inconsistency. Always state: paragraph, bullet list, table, JSON, etc.

❌ Too many tasks at once

One prompt, one task. Cramming five requests in one prompt reduces quality on all of them.

Remember: Higher temperature ≠ smarter. For accuracy-critical tasks, use 0 or low temperature.
