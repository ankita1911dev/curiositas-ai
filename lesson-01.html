What is AI, Really?

A plain-English explanation — no jargon, no coding, no experience needed. By the end of this lesson you will be able to explain AI to anyone.

Let's start with a story

Imagine you have a friend — let's call him Max. Max has spent the last 10 years doing nothing but reading. Every book ever written. Every Wikipedia article. Every recipe, every news story, every tweet, every forum post. Billions and billions of pages of text.

Because Max has read so much, he has become incredibly good at one thing: finishing sentences. You say "The capital of France is…" and Max immediately says "Paris." You say "To make a cup of tea, first you…" and Max says "boil the water."

Max does not think the way you think. He does not understand the world the way you do. He just recognises patterns from everything he has read — and uses those patterns to predict what word should come next.

That is exactly what an AI language model like ChatGPT or Claude does. It read a huge amount of human writing, and learned to predict what word should come next. That's it. Everything else — answering questions, writing essays, solving problems — comes from that one ability, applied at incredible scale and speed.

So what does "AI" actually mean?

AI stands for Artificial Intelligence. It is a broad term for computer programs that can do things that normally require human intelligence — like understanding language, recognising faces in photos, playing chess, or having a conversation.

There are many types of AI. The ones you probably use every day are called Large Language Models (LLMs). Examples include:

  • ChatGPT — made by OpenAI
  • Claude — made by Anthropic
  • Gemini — made by Google
  • Copilot — made by Microsoft

They are all different, but they all work in basically the same way: they were trained on enormous amounts of text, and they generate responses by predicting words one at a time.

Think of an LLM like an incredibly well-read person who has never left the library. They know an enormous amount — but everything they know came from reading. They have never touched a hot stove, or felt rain, or smelled coffee. They only know things through words.

How did AI learn all of this?

The training process works roughly like this:

  1. Engineers collected a massive amount of text from the internet, books, and other sources — think hundreds of billions of words.
  2. They fed that text to a computer program and said: "Read a sentence. Predict the next word. Check if you were right. If you were wrong, adjust yourself slightly."
  3. They repeated that process trillions of times — until the model got very, very good at predicting text.
  4. Then they used human feedback to teach it to be helpful, honest, and safe.

The result? A system that can write poetry, explain quantum physics, debug code, translate languages, summarise books, and have conversations — all because it got extremely good at predicting the right next word.

If AI just predicts the next word — how can it seem so intelligent? The answer is that human language is incredibly information-dense. To predict the next word correctly in a complex sentence, you actually need to model a lot of real-world knowledge. Language and intelligence are more connected than we think.

What AI is NOT

This is just as important as knowing what AI is:

  • AI is not conscious. It does not have feelings, opinions, or experiences. When it says "I think…" it is using a pattern from its training — not expressing genuine thought.
  • AI does not know what is true. It can confidently state something completely wrong. This is called a hallucination — and you will learn more about it in Lesson 4.
  • AI has no memory between conversations (unless it is given one). Every new chat starts from scratch.
  • AI does not browse the internet in real time (unless it has a special tool for that). Its knowledge was frozen at the time it was trained.
The most important thing to understand about AI: it is a pattern-matching machine trained on human text. It is incredibly powerful and useful — but it is not magic, not intelligent in the human sense, and not always right.

Why should you care?

Here is the honest truth: AI tools are already changing how people work, study, and create. People who know how to use AI well are becoming more productive, more creative, and more effective.

This is not about replacing humans. It is about having a powerful tool — like a calculator for your brain — that you know how to use well. And just like calculators, the people who understand them best get the most out of them.

That is what this site is for. In the next lesson, we will look at tokens — the building blocks that AI uses to read and write. It is strange, fascinating, and very different from how you read.
